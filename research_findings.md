## 1. Overview & Definition  

| Term | Typical Definition (2024‑2026) |
|------|-------------------------------|
| **AI Agent** | An autonomous or semi‑autonomous software system that can perceive its environment, reason toward a goal, and take actions (including tool‑calls, API invocations, or physical actuation) without continuous human prompting. |
| **Agentic AI** | A subset of generative AI that adds “agency” – i.e., the ability to plan, execute multi‑step workflows, and maintain state across interactions. |
| **Autonomy Levels** | 1️⃣ Assistive (human‑in‑the‑loop) → 2️⃣ Task‑oriented (single‑goal) → 3️⃣ Goal‑oriented (multiple sub‑goals) → 4️⃣ Fully autonomous (self‑directed, self‑monitoring).  (Source: Knight Columbia “Levels of Autonomy for AI Agents”, 2025). |

## 7. Risks, Challenges & Governance

| Challenge | Details | Mitigation / Recommendations |
|-----------|---------|------------------------------|
| **Hallucination & Incorrect Action** | Agents may generate erroneous commands (e.g., wrong financial transaction). | Human‑in‑the‑loop approvals, verification APIs, continuous monitoring. |
| **Security & Privacy** | Agents with broad tool access can exfiltrate data. | Principle of least privilege, sandboxed execution environments, audit logs. |
| **Regulatory Uncertainty** | No unified global standards for autonomous agents. | Adopt ISO/IEC 42001 (AI governance) and sector‑specific guidelines (e.g., FINRA for finance). |
| **Model Drift & Degradation** | Over time, performance may degrade as data distributions shift. | Periodic retraining, automated performance alerts, “model‑as‑a‑service” updates. |
| **Talent Gap** | Shortage of engineers skilled in agentic AI (prompt‑engineering, tool‑integration). | Low‑code platforms, up‑skilling programs, community‑driven open‑source contributions (LangChain, LangGraph). |
